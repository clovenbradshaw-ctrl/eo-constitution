# EO Constitution

The one governing document of the EOReader lineage. Everything that ships —
an organ, a prior, a host, a deletion — ships into one of four domains, and
this document decides which. The oracle in `oracle/` is its enforcement:
a placement claim that does not pass the articles does not ship.

**The mission is the invariant:** to joyfully experience meaningful surprise —
an earned, measured, faithful encounter with a source. Every placement
decision is in service of that. A ruling that weakens the encounter is a
misreading of this document, even when the letter of an article permits it.

**The one operation is the physiology:** a figure is difference against a
ground you rebuild; pattern is the difference the figure makes; level is
another figure's ground. The repos are the three terms plus the surfaces
where the difference is experienced.

---

## Article I — The domain

**I.1 The engine is `eoreader6`.** It is the measurement: the current,
re-earned implementation of the one operation, governed by `eoreader6/SEED.md`.

**I.2 `eoreader5` and `eoreader4.2` are legacy.** Frozen reference: the
record of what was tried and measured. Their dead-end logs are load-bearing
and must be trusted. Nothing is ported from them; every organ is re-earned in
eoreader6 or it does not come. A legacy organ that has not been re-earned
does not exist for placement purposes.

**I.3 Priors are `eoPriors`.** The ground: what cannot be measured must be
received. A prior is a gift and must name its giver.

**I.4 Applications are thin hosts** (`eoreader-chat`, `eoreader-proxy`,
`eoreaderapp`, `eoreader-mcp`). The surfaces of encounter. They own everything
the engine must not: the clock, I/O, persistence, model routing, and the
interface. Deleting an application must change no engine reading.

**I.5 No other domain exists.** A thing that fits none of the four is a gap,
not a new category. The four domains are not a menu; they are the consequence
of the one operation.

## Article II — The routing tests

Ask in this order. **Prior? → App? → what remains is engine.** A claim is
routed by the first test that answers it.

**II.1 The omnimodal test.** *Would a leitmotif in a symphony have this
problem?* If a mechanism can only be stated in terms of a name string, a
surface, or a language-specific rule, it is not engine. Text's stable spans
are a false permanency.

**II.2 The giver test.** Knowledge about the material — who a name denotes,
that two words name one thing, what a narrator calls someone, which words
bind a relation — is witness knowledge and belongs in priors, and it must
name its giver. **A missing giver is a wall, not a gap-in-waiting:** report a
typed gap, never derive. Three independent derivation mechanisms collapsed
toward the material's own vocabulary at r ≈ 0.974.

**II.3 The host test.** Knowledge about the reader, the host, the moment, or
the interface — the clock, files, the network, the model being called, the
session, the layout — belongs in the application.

**II.4 Invariance.** What survives every text and every host is engine. What
could have been otherwise in a different text is priors. What could have been
otherwise in a different deployment is app.

**II.5 Two-tier refusal.** Type error before null. If the placement cannot be
stated, nothing ships and no measurement is spent. A claim that is not
machine-routable is not a hard problem — it is a wall.

## Article III — The seams

**III.1 Derive vs receive.** Model-tier knowledge is injected as priors,
never imported. It reranks what the engine surfaced; it never retrieves on
its own. Where the engine surfaced nothing, it returns a typed gap instead
of a memory.

**III.2 Pure vs host.** The engine has no clock, no I/O, no randomness, no
surface, and holds no prior. The host supplies `ts`/`seq`; the engine has
none. A reaction is an observation of a reader, not an inference — it never
carries an operator, a prior id, or an epoch.

**III.3 A missing prior is a typed gap, never a silently wrong number.**

## Article IV — Amendment

**IV.1 The constitution is amendable.** An amendment is a versioned change
to this text that updates the enforcement tests in the same change.
Amendments change the test, visibly; an amendment that cannot be expressed
as a changed failing test is not an amendment, it is an exception.

**IV.2 The oracle proposes and checks; it never amends.** Amendment is a
human act. Agents propose, humans dispose — the same rule as the exemplar
basis.

**IV.3 The growth rule governs additions.** A new organ joins the engine only
when the level test returns `above` against the core. `peer` or `unstable`
means it waits. Unwired is failing — a module nothing depends on is not early,
it is refuted.

**IV.4 Every verdict cites the articles that produced it.** A ruling without
a trace does not ship.

**IV.5 Editing this file is allowed. It makes this a different project.**
Consequences are revisable; they are not grandfathered.
