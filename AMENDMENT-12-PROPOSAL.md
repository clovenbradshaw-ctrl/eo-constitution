# Proposed 12th amendment — the signal-preservation test (II.17)

**Status: DRAFT PROPOSAL. Not applied, not yet human-reviewed.** IV.2 — the
assay proposes and checks, it never amends; amendment is a human act. This
document is an agent's drafting of a defect caught twice, live, in a single
working session on `eoreader6` (2026-08-06) — not a conversion of a prior
item from another document, unlike Amendments 8–11.

---

## The defect

Two instances, in one session, on the same piece of work, in opposite
directions:

**First — a reused mechanism silently imported an undisclosed axis
requirement.** Building a role-discovery experiment
(`scripts/experiments/role-fold-kinds.mjs`), candidate (subject, verb,
object) fillers were generated by reusing
`perceiver/text/relations.js::extractRelations` — already built, already
declared weak *at its own site* ("This is still a heuristic, and still
declared as such"). Pointed at a new consumer (`induceKinds`, an organ built
specifically to avoid hand-typed grammar), that same declared weakness
became a new, undisclosed defect: a hand-typed, contiguous
subject-verb-object requirement quietly became the candidate-generation
step of what was presented as a *discovered* role hypergraph. The cost was
invisible in the code — the import line reads as ordinary reuse — and
visible only in the output: discourse markers ("As," "Once," "See") and
citation fragments ("5 U," "at https://www") admitted as false "subject" and
"object" fillers, because the regex does not know the difference between a
grammatical role and a token that happens to sit next to a verb it
recognizes.

**Second — the correction overshot into discarding a different axis.**
Asked *why is there regex at all*, the proposed fix was to drop
subject/object labeling and word-order sensitivity entirely — treating a
filler's position before or after its verb as symmetric. This would have
discarded real signal: word order correlates with grammatical role often
enough in English (and is load-bearing in many languages) that removing
order-sensitivity to fix an *order-over-reliance* defect is the same class
of error, mirror-imaged. Caught before it was built, not after.

Both instances share one shape: a mechanism was reached for or reshaped
because it was cheap, available, or seemed to fix the immediately visible
problem — and in both directions, an axis the phenomenon actually depends on
would have been discarded, silently, as a side effect of a decision made for
convenience rather than for what the phenomenon needed. The pattern is
recurring within a single session, which is a stronger signal than a single
instance, not a weaker one — it suggests this is a standing failure mode of
fast iterative building, not a one-off.

## Why no existing article catches this

- **II.9 (revision)**'s "a cheap sense organ is legal and useful; a cheap
  sense organ promoted to the verdict is refused" is close but answers a
  different question — it is about a cheap heuristic being mistaken for the
  final significance judgment. It says nothing about a cheap heuristic's own
  internal narrowing compounding when its output is fed into a *different*
  mechanism than the one it was built for.
- **II.11 / II.13 (omnimodal / script earning)** require disclosing scope —
  medium, script — as a received giver, or refusing the claim of generality.
  Real ground, but narrower than the general case: an SVO regex's
  word-order requirement is not a medium-scope or script-scope fact, it is
  an axis-of-the-phenomenon fact, and nothing routes that.
- **II.15 (draft, validation discipline)** asks whether a claim that a
  heuristic predicts a real outcome was checked against enough ground truth.
  Adjacent — both are about not letting convenience masquerade as
  correctness — but II.15 is about *sample size behind a predictive claim*.
  This defect is present even with a large, well-measured sample: the SVO
  regex's failure mode is systematic, not a small-N artifact, and no amount
  of additional pocket data would have surfaced or fixed it — only reading
  the actual triples did.
- **Nothing existing** asks the reuse question directly: when mechanism B
  consumes mechanism A's output, does A's own declared narrowness (legitimate
  at A's site) transfer cleanly to B's purpose, or does it need a fresh
  accounting because B was built to avoid exactly what A quietly reintroduces.

## Proposed article

> **II.17 The signal-preservation test.** *Does a chosen simplification —
> reused for cost, speed, or convenience — discard an axis of variation the
> phenomenon actually depends on, and if it does, is that loss disclosed and
> measured, rather than inherited silently by whatever reads its output
> next?*
>
> Every real mechanism narrows something, and a declared, weak matcher
> (relations.js's own SVO extractor, admitted as such at its own site) is
> legitimate exactly because the narrowing is named where it is made.
> This article refuses narrowing that is not named at the site where it
> starts mattering — which is not always the site where it was written.
> Two named consequences:
>
> - **Reuse re-opens the accounting.** A mechanism's declared narrowness at
>   its own site of definition does not travel automatically to a new site
>   that consumes its output. Feeding a hand-typed SVO extractor's triples
>   into a discovery organ built specifically to avoid hand-typed grammar is
>   a new claim, not a reuse of an old one, and needs its own disclosure of
>   what axis the borrowed mechanism still does not model.
> - **The fix for a discarded axis is not discarding a different one.**
>   Correcting a mechanism that over-relied on one axis (order, completeness,
>   contiguity) by removing sensitivity to that axis entirely repeats the
>   defect from the other side. The axis being thrown away changed; the
>   throwing away did not. The correct repair keeps the axis as a *measured
>   feature* available to whatever reads it, without imposing it as a *hard
>   requirement* or a *pre-assigned label*.

## Proposed enforcement

Harder to reduce to a single evidence boolean than II.1–II.14, same
difficulty II.15 already names — this is a claim about *how a reuse
decision was made*, not about one mechanism's own shape. Two possible
shapes, left for the human disposing this proposal to choose between:

1. **A required disclosure comment at the reuse site.** Any module that
   feeds one organ's output into a different organ than the one it was
   built for must name, at the point of reuse, which axis of the
   phenomenon the borrowed organ does not model and why that gap is
   acceptable for the new purpose — the same discipline
   `goldens/agency-civic/provenance.json` already applies to corpus
   sourcing, extended to code reuse.
2. **A conformance-only enforcement**: `scripts/experiments/` (and any
   future promotion path out of it) requires a companion note, checked for
   presence but not content, naming the borrowed organ's declared
   narrowness and whether the new use inherits it unchanged, widens it, or
   is newly exposed to it.

No exemplar claim or code diff is proposed here, matching II.15's own
precedent for an enforcement-shape-open article.

>   over-reliance on one axis by discarding sensitivity to it entirely is
>   the same defect, not its repair. A mechanism's declared narrowness at
>   its own site does not travel automatically to a new site that reuses
>   its output; reuse re-opens the accounting. [Enforcement mechanism TBD by
>   human disposal — see the two proposed shapes above.]

## What it would have caught

Both instances above, at the point they were made rather than the point
they were noticed: `role-fold-kinds.mjs`'s first version would have needed
a comment naming that `extractRelations` requires contiguous SVO order and
does not model any other construction, before it shipped as a "discovered"
role hypergraph's candidate generator — which would likely have prompted
the position-window redesign immediately, without an intervening noisy run.
And the order-agnostic over-correction would have needed to name which axis
it was newly discarding (order-as-signal) before being proposed, which is
exactly the check that in fact caught it — one turn later, by a human, not
by an article.

## Open question, recorded rather than settled

Is disclosure enough, or does this need a measured cost (II.15-style: run
both the narrowed and unnarrowed version, report the delta) before a reused
mechanism is trusted at a new site? The two instances here were both caught
by inspection and argument, not by a measurement of what the narrowing
actually cost in either direction — which is itself possibly a gap this
amendment does not yet close.
